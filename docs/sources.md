# Sources and retained versions

## 1. Economic argument and identification

The current paper uses the original US narrative policy record and monthly household purchase quantities. The literature comparison supports a project-specific finite-horizon assessment, rather than a claim that durable-demand reversal, cumulative purchase accounting or projection algebra is new.

## 3. Data definitions and provenance

The [US source manifest](../data/raw/2026-10-11-us-purchases/manifest.json) retains original source bytes, retrieval details and hashes. The [declared design](us_purchase_design.md) records the common sample, outcome timing and inference rules; the informed diagnostics have a separate specification. Earlier German evidence remains in the repository history and retained research records.

The original [Romer–Romer workbook](https://eml.berkeley.edu/~cromer/Data/Shocks/RomerandRomerDataAppendix.xls) and [data descriptions](https://eml.berkeley.edu/~cromer/Data/Shocks/RomerandRomerDataDescriptions.pdf) are the primary author versions associated with the 2004 paper. The workbook SHA-256 is `4f90c85125443e67177e410245539fc9cec3b9cf22db978ed72463ed63eab24e`. All nineteen source-purge columns are reproduced. Missing purges and pre-coverage placeholder zeros are distinguished from measured policy inaction.

BEA's *NIPA Handbook*, chapter 5, December 2024, [pp. 5-2–5-6](https://www.bea.gov/resources/methodologies/nipa-handbook/pdf/chapter-05.pdf), supplies PCE scope and monthly table definitions. Its [chained-dollar](https://www.bea.gov/help/glossary/chained-dollar-estimates) and [annual-rate](https://www.bea.gov/help/glossary/annual-rates) definitions support the within-category flow normalisation. Retained quantity series are `DDURRA3M086SBEA`, `DNDGRA3M086SBEA` and `DSERRA3M086SBEA`; state controls include `DSPIC96`, `PCEPI` and `FEDFUNDS`. These official series are distributed by FRED in the 11 October 2026 vintage. Chained categories are not added into a synthetic aggregate.

The observed financial benchmark uses the Federal Reserve Board's [daily DFF series](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/DFF), retaining published calendar-day observations. The Board's [September 1973 Bulletin](https://fraser.stlouisfed.org/title/federal-reserve-bulletin-62/september-1973-21466/fulltext), p. A33, footnote 3, documents the historical rate-method change; [H.15 definitions](https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/h15/) explain calendar-day averaging and the later 2016 median convention. Historical observation dates are not shifted to publication dates. Journal article texts are not redistributed in this repository.
